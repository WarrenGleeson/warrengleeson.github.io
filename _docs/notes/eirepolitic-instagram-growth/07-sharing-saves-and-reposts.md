# Sharing, Saves, and Reposts

## Status

**Planning state:** detailed investigation/experiment guide prepared.  
**Production changes:** none.  
**Priority:** high.  
**Dependencies:** Insights/Measurement should provide share/save/repost metrics; Discovery Hooks and Topical/Evergreen determine which formats are easiest to distribute; Profile Conversion captures downstream follower value.

## Objective

Increase EirePolitic's organic distribution by making posts useful enough that people naturally:

- send them to someone else;
- repost them;
- save them for reference;
- return to them later;
- visit the EirePolitic profile after receiving them from another person.

The core strategy is **utility-driven distribution**, not outrage-driven engagement.

For EirePolitic, the ideal growth loop is:

```text
accurate political-information post
        ↓
viewer finds it useful / clarifying
        ↓
viewer sends, reposts, or saves it
        ↓
new non-followers see the original EirePolitic post
        ↓
new viewer understands the post without prior context
        ↓
profile visit / follow / repeat engagement
```

# Current Instagram platform context

These observations were reviewed in October 2026 and should be rechecked before implementation because platform mechanics change.

## Native reposts create a genuine distribution surface

Instagram introduced native reposts for public feed posts and Reels in 2025.

Meta states that:

- reposted public posts/Reels remain credited to the original creator;
- reposts can appear in the reposter's profile repost tab;
- reposted content may be recommended to the reposter's friends/followers;
- this can expose the original creator to people who do not already follow them.

For EirePolitic, this makes **repostability** a real acquisition objective rather than merely a vanity interaction.

## Sharing is relevant to recommendation systems

Meta has publicly described predictions such as whether a person is likely to share a post as one of many signals used by recommendation systems.

Do not interpret this as a simple formula such as “more shares = guaranteed reach.”

The practical implication is narrower:

**content that people genuinely find worth sending is aligned with Instagram's concept of valuable content and can also create direct person-to-person distribution.**

## Original content remains strategically favourable

Meta reported in January 2026 that 75% of Instagram recommendations in the US were coming from original posts after increasing the prevalence of original material.

EirePolitic's structured transformations of official political data are original content and should remain clearly branded/source-attributed.

## Saves should be treated as utility evidence

This plan does **not** assume that saves mechanically increase reach.

Use saves primarily as evidence that a post has:

- reference value;
- long-term utility;
- revisit potential;
- educational value.

A saved post may be strategically valuable even when its immediate reach is modest.

# Core strategic hypothesis

EirePolitic is unusually well suited to utility-driven sharing because many of its potential posts answer questions people routinely need explained:

- what a Bill does;
- where a Bill is in the process;
- what a parliamentary stage means;
- how a Dáil vote should be interpreted;
- what changed in polling;
- how a constituency/representative system works;
- what recorded political data does and does not show.

These subjects do not require controversy to become shareable.

The strongest distribution question should therefore be:

> “Would someone send or repost this because it helps another person understand something?”

rather than:

> “How can this post provoke more reactions?”

# Three distinct behaviours

Do not treat shares, saves, and reposts as interchangeable.

## 1. Sends / shares

Likely user intent:

- “You were asking about this.”
- “This explains the thing we were discussing.”
- “Look at this result/change.”
- “This is useful context.”

Best content properties:

- topical relevance;
- simple explanation;
- self-contained first slide;
- clear factual result;
- low jargon.

Primary growth value:

**direct exposure to new people.**

## 2. Saves

Likely user intent:

- “I may need this later.”
- “This is a useful reference.”
- “I want to compare future posts with this.”
- “This explains a process I forget.”

Best content properties:

- evergreen reference value;
- glossary/process explanation;
- charts/tables that repay revisiting;
- clean source/methodology.

Primary growth value:

**repeat utility and account credibility.**

## 3. Native reposts

Likely user intent:

- “I want my followers/friends to see this.”
- “This represents useful information I am comfortable redistributing.”

Best content properties:

- self-contained;
- clearly attributable;
- neutral enough that reposting it does not imply a partisan endorsement;
- visually legible in feeds;
- useful to a broader audience than the original follower base.

Primary growth value:

**attributed exposure through another user's network.**

# “Repost-safe” design standard

A repost-safe EirePolitic post should pass all of these tests.

## 1. Understandable without knowing EirePolitic

The first slide should tell a new viewer:

- what the topic is;
- what kind of information is being shown;
- the relevant date/period where needed.

Do not rely on a series name alone.

## 2. Visible original attribution

EirePolitic identity should be present but not dominate the content.

Because native reposts credit the original poster, branding does not need to be oversized.

However, screenshots and off-platform sharing may strip Instagram UI attribution, so important slides should contain modest visible EirePolitic/source identity.

## 3. No missing critical context

The post should not become misleading when encountered outside the original follower's context.

Examples:

- polling must identify that it is polling;
- speech frequency must not look like party position;
- amendment vote must not look like a vote on the whole Bill;
- chart units/period must remain visible.

## 4. Neutral redistribution risk

Another neutral/civic account should be able to repost the item without appearing to endorse a partisan argument.

This favours:

- explainers;
- timelines;
- procedural information;
- sourced descriptive data;
- clearly labelled polling;
- factual Bill summaries.

## 5. Durable link between hook and evidence

A viewer should be able to inspect later slides/caption and verify the first-slide claim.

## 6. Mobile readability

Reposted material must remain readable without zooming.

Small methodology text should not be the only place where a material limitation appears.

# “Save-worthy” design standard

A save-worthy post should provide durable reference value.

Use one or more of these properties.

## Reference compression

Take something complicated and compress it into a reusable guide.

Examples:

- Bill stages in one carousel;
- Dáil division terminology;
- polling percentage-points explainer;
- parliamentary-question types.

## Future comparability

The post becomes more useful when later data arrives.

Examples:

- six-month polling snapshot;
- baseline party-speech breakdown;
- legislative timeline.

## Navigation value

The post helps a user understand future EirePolitic content.

Examples:

- how to read Bill Tracker;
- what EirePolitic means by recorded-vote participation;
- how speech classification works.

## Practical lookup value

The post answers a question the user may need again.

Examples:

- who represents a constituency, when that product exists;
- what each Bill stage means;
- what happens after a Bill passes a chamber.

# Existing / planned EirePolitic formats ranked by likely behaviour

These are hypotheses to test, not performance claims.

## High save potential

### Parliamentary-process explainers

Examples:

- `How a Bill becomes law in Ireland`;
- `What does Second Stage mean?`;
- `How to read a Dáil division`;
- `Why an amendment vote is not always a vote on the entire Bill`.

Why:

They solve recurring knowledge gaps and remain useful for future posts.

### Data-literacy explainers

Examples:

- `Percentage points vs percentage change`;
- `What speech-frequency data measures`;
- `What recorded-vote participation does and does not mean`.

Why:

They are durable references and improve comprehension of future EirePolitic data.

### Representative / constituency references

When suitable products exist:

- constituency representative guides;
- institutional structure guides.

Why:

Practical lookup value.

## High send/share potential

### Plain-language Bill changes

Examples:

- `What this new law changes`;
- `3 laws passed this session — what each one changes`.

Why:

Easy to send to someone interested in a particular policy subject.

### “What changed?” polling posts

Why:

Simple, topical, quickly understood.

### Topical explainer bridges

Example:

`This Bill just reached Second Stage — here's what that actually means.`

Why:

Combines current relevance with educational utility.

### Procedural surprises

Example:

`This Bill progressed without a recorded member-by-member division — here's how that can happen.`

Why:

Contains genuine curiosity without manufactured controversy.

## High native-repost potential

### Self-contained neutral charts

- polling snapshot with methodology;
- parliamentary timeline;
- clearly labelled descriptive issue data.

### Evergreen civic explainers

A civic/student/media/community account can redistribute these without adopting a political position.

### Public-record summary cards

Examples:

- Bill status timeline;
- plain-language legislative change;
- factual Oireachtas process card.

---

# Formats less likely to justify deliberate share/save optimisation

## Routine internal-series updates

A post whose main message is only:

`Post 3 of this month's series`

has little inherent reason to send/save.

Improve the information proposition rather than adding a “share this” CTA.

## Dense methodology-only content

Methodology can be important, but forcing every methodology post to become a mass-distribution asset is unnecessary.

Some content should optimise for trust, not reach.

## Highly contextual party-by-party slides

These may perform well with people already following politics but can carry higher partisan-interpretation risk when isolated.

Ensure labels/caveats remain visible before encouraging redistribution.

# Distribution-oriented carousel architecture

## Slide 1 — send/repost entry point

Must be understandable to a recipient who has never seen EirePolitic.

Use:

- plain-language topic;
- key question/result;
- relevant date/period;
- modest EirePolitic branding.

## Slide 2 — immediate value

Deliver the answer quickly.

Do not make a shared recipient swipe through branding before receiving the promised information.

## Middle slides — evidence/context

Add:

- source-backed explanation;
- chart/detail;
- formal title;
- vote breakdown;
- comparison.

## Final slide — reference utility

Depending on content:

- source/methodology;
- glossary;
- “how to interpret this”;
- related evergreen explainer;
- one appropriate CTA.

---

# Standalone summary card

For some long carousels, consider adding a compact **summary/reference slide** that can stand alone when screenshotted or shared externally.

Example Bill Tracker summary card:

```text
THREE LAWS PASSED THIS SESSION

Energy security — [short factual change]
Settlement goods — [short factual change]
AI regulation — [short factual change]

Source: Houses of the Oireachtas
EirePolitic
```

This is not required for every carousel.

Test whether it creates enough share/save value to justify the extra slide.

Do not reduce complex Bills to one-line summaries if the line becomes misleading.

# Calls to action

Use CTAs only where the content naturally supports them.

## Save CTA

Suitable:

- `Save this guide for the next time a Bill reaches Second Stage.`
- `Save this as a reference for reading Dáil divisions.`

Not suitable:

- every routine polling post;
- content with little future reference value.

## Send/share CTA

Suitable:

- `Send this to someone following this Bill.`
- `Share this with someone who wants a plain-language guide to the Oireachtas process.`

The sentence should explain why sharing would help.

## Repost CTA

Use cautiously.

A neutral informational account should not repeatedly ask everyone to repost content.

Potentially appropriate on especially reusable public-information material:

- `Repost this guide if it would be useful to your audience.`

This is more appropriate for an evergreen civic explainer than a party-specific political-data post.

## No CTA

If the content is already strong and there is no natural action, omit the CTA.

---

# Native repost vs screenshot sharing

Design for both.

## Native repost

Advantages:

- original creator attribution retained;
- interaction remains attached to the original content;
- repost can potentially expose EirePolitic to the reposter's followers.

Preferred when users want to redistribute the whole post.

## Screenshot/off-platform sharing

Likely for:

- WhatsApp;
- Signal;
- X/Bluesky;
- group chats;
- articles/newsletters;
- presentations/education.

Implication:

Every high-share asset should retain enough embedded attribution/context to survive outside Instagram.

Do not use watermarks that obstruct the actual information.

---

# Source attribution as a sharing asset

Visible sourcing increases the probability that a journalist/student/civic account can reuse a post responsibly.

Use a compact source layer such as:

- `Source: Houses of the Oireachtas`;
- polling organisation/date;
- EirePolitic methodology indicator.

Where a source is complex, the caption/public methodology page can carry the detailed reference.

The image itself should still identify the source class clearly enough to avoid appearing unsourced.

---

# “Shareability without neutrality loss” checklist

Before a post is deliberately promoted as shareable/repostable, verify:

- [ ] headline is descriptive rather than partisan;
- [ ] no actor is praised/attacked;
- [ ] no inferred motive;
- [ ] no election prediction;
- [ ] no speech-frequency-to-position inference;
- [ ] exact vote proposition is represented correctly;
- [ ] relevant period/date visible;
- [ ] source visible or immediately accessible;
- [ ] EirePolitic attribution survives screenshotting;
- [ ] first slide can be understood in isolation;
- [ ] any essential caveat remains visible when redistributed;
- [ ] CTA does not manufacture political conflict.

# Audience-use-case framing

Do not build demographic or ideological targeting models.

Instead design around **use cases**.

## “Help me understand”

Best content:

- process explainers;
- terminology;
- Bill summaries.

Likely action:

save/share.

## “I am following this issue”

Best content:

- topical Bill change;
- polling change;
- relevant official-record summary.

Likely action:

send/follow.

## “I need a reference”

Best content:

- timeline;
- representative guide;
- glossary;
- methodology explainer.

Likely action:

save.

## “My audience would find this useful”

Best content:

- neutral self-contained explainer/chart.

Likely action:

native repost.

This is more aligned with EirePolitic's neutrality than political-segment targeting.

# Experiment programme

## SR-001 — utility CTA vs no CTA

### Question

Does an appropriate save/share CTA materially increase the requested action?

### Candidate subject

Evergreen Bill-stage explainer.

### Control

No CTA.

### Treatment

`Save this guide for the next time a Bill reaches Second Stage.`

### Primary metric

Saves per 1,000 reached.

### Secondary

- follows per 1,000 reached;
- shares;
- reach.

Do not use likes as the decision metric.

---

## SR-002 — self-contained summary slide

### Question

Does adding a standalone summary/reference slide increase saves/shares enough to justify extra carousel depth?

### Control

Existing carousel.

### Treatment

Same carousel + compact summary/reference slide.

### Metrics

- saves per 1,000 reached;
- shares per 1,000 reached;
- completion/carousel depth where available;
- follow conversion.

Reject if the extra slide reduces consumption without increasing utility.

---

## SR-003 — source prominence

### Question

Does clearer visible sourcing improve redistribution/utility without cluttering the design?

Compare:

- source only in caption/footer;
- compact source visible on summary/cover/detail card.

Primary interest:

- shares;
- saves;
- reposts where metric exposed.

This may also be evaluated qualitatively with collaboration partners later.

---

## SR-004 — topical sendability

Compare two topical packaging approaches using equivalent data:

Control:

procedural/status framing.

Treatment:

plain-language “what changed / what it means” framing.

Primary metric:

shares per 1,000 reached.

Secondary:

non-follower reach and follows.

This overlaps deliberately with Discovery Hooks; use one experiment record rather than double-counting if the same publications test both hypotheses.

---

## SR-005 — evergreen saveability

Compare evergreen explainers with recurring procedural posts.

Question:

Do evergreen reference posts produce a larger save rate and stronger 30-day long-tail reach?

Use medians across multiple posts rather than one-off comparisons.

---

## SR-006 — native-repost candidate

Select one genuinely reusable public-information post.

Do **not** ask a broad partisan network to repost it.

Instead:

- publish normally;
- optionally use a restrained repost CTA;
- observe native repost metric if available;
- record downstream non-follower reach/profile visits.

If native reposts are rare but shares/sends are strong, optimise for sends instead of forcing repost behaviour.

# Metrics

## Primary distribution metrics

### Shares per 1,000 reached

```text
(shares / reach) * 1000
```

### Saves per 1,000 reached

```text
(saves / reach) * 1000
```

### Reposts per 1,000 reached

```text
(reposts / reach) * 1000
```

only where Meta exposes reliable repost data for the media/account.

## Downstream acquisition metrics

- non-follower reach;
- profile visits per 1,000 reached;
- follows per 1,000 reached;
- follower growth around high-distribution posts.

Do not claim a repost/share caused a follow unless Meta exposes the attribution or the experiment design supports the inference.

## Long-tail metric

For reference content:

```text
30-day reach / 7-day reach
```

and retained save count.

This helps identify posts that keep providing value after the initial publication window.

# Repost / share candidate scoring

Do not create a political “importance” score.

A lightweight operational candidate score may consider only:

- self-contained comprehension;
- source completeness;
- evergreen/reference utility;
- freshness;
- existing template availability;
- low semantic risk;
- topic duplication/cooldown;
- historical share/save performance of the format.

The score answers:

> “Is this suitable for a distribution-oriented treatment?”

not:

> “How politically important is this?”

# Automation opportunities

## 1. Automatic shareability classification

Based on content family and template.

Examples:

```text
Second Stage explainer → evergreen_save
poll movement → topical_send
Bill “what changed” → topical_send
Dáil division glossary → evergreen_save / repost_reference
```

This can select CTA/template behaviour automatically.

## 2. Automatic CTA selection

Map content intent to approved CTA IDs.

Do not use generative free-text CTA writing for routine posts when deterministic approved phrases are sufficient.

## 3. Automatic source/footer layer

Pull source labels from existing provenance metadata.

## 4. Reusable summary-card renderer

For selected long carousels, generate a final compact reference slide from approved structured fields.

## 5. Performance feedback

Insights should report:

- share/save/repost rates by template;
- by content family;
- by topicality;
- by CTA;
- by summary-card presence.

Then retire low-performing tactics.

- keep source attribution visible;
- keep EirePolitic attribution unobtrusive but persistent;

# Risks and safeguards

## Engagement bait

Do not create polarising prompts solely to increase comments/shares.

## Partisan redistribution

A neutral EirePolitic post may still be selectively reposted by partisan accounts.

That does not automatically compromise EirePolitic, but the original content must remain neutral and self-contained so its meaning is not dependent on the reposter's commentary.

Do not alter editorial framing to appeal to a specific ideological network.

## Context stripping

Design important slides to survive screenshotting/reposting without losing crucial labels.

## Over-branding

Do not turn reference graphics into advertisements. The information should remain primary.

## Misreading share metrics

A high share count can arise from controversy as well as utility.

Review:

- comments/context;
- profile conversion;
- non-follower reach;
- semantic accuracy;

before declaring a format successful.

## Save over-optimisation

A highly saved glossary post may not be the best acquisition post. Keep saves as one signal among several.

## CTA fatigue

Do not attach “save/share/repost” to every post.

# Completion criteria

This workstream is mature when:

- EirePolitic has explicit design standards for sendable, saveable, and repostable content;
- several reusable evergreen reference assets exist;
- topical summaries are self-contained enough for distribution outside the follower base;
- source/attribution survives native reposts and screenshot sharing;
- CTAs are selected according to actual utility rather than added mechanically;
- Insights measures share/save/repost rates and downstream acquisition;
- high-performing distribution treatments can be applied automatically;
- recurring manual solicitation is unnecessary;
- growth does not depend on controversy or partisan engagement farming.

The target end state is **content that travels because it is useful enough to pass from one person to another, while remaining recognisably and reliably EirePolitic wherever it is encountered**.
