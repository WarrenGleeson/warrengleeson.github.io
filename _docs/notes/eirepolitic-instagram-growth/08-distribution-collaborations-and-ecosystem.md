# Distribution, Collaborations, and Ecosystem

## Status

**Planning state:** detailed investigation/implementation guide prepared.  
**Production changes:** none.  
**Priority:** medium-high after measurement, packaging, and shareability foundations are in place.  
**Primary constraint:** distribution should add audience without creating a recurring outreach job or compromising EirePolitic's neutrality.

## Objective

Reach relevant people outside EirePolitic's existing follower base by using a small number of credible distribution relationships and native Instagram sharing/collaboration mechanisms.

The strategy should favour:

- no-cost distribution;
- very low outreach volume;
- reusable partner relationships;
- posts that partners want to redistribute because they are useful;
- neutral civic/educational overlap;
- measurable follower acquisition;
- minimal recurring manual effort.

The strategy should **not** depend on:

- mass DMs;
- daily commenting;
- reciprocal engagement groups;
- automated unsolicited outreach;
- political-party partnerships;
- influencer sponsorships;
- paid amplification;
- ideological coalition-building.

---

# Core distribution model

Use a ladder of increasing manual effort.

```text
1. Make content inherently redistributable
        ↓
2. Earn organic sends/reposts without outreach
        ↓
3. Use relevant mentions/tags where editorially justified
        ↓
4. Make occasional targeted repost/share requests
        ↓
5. Run selective native Collab posts
        ↓
6. Develop recurring distribution relationships only when proven
```

EirePolitic should spend most of its time at levels 1-3.

Levels 4-6 are experiments, not default operating requirements.

# Current Instagram platform context

These points were reviewed in October 2026 and should be rechecked before implementation.

## Instagram Collabs

Meta documents Instagram Collabs as a way to co-author feed posts, carousels, and Reels.

Current documented capability allows the originating account to invite **up to three collaborators**.

Once accepted, the content can be shown on the collaborators' profiles and distributed to their audiences while retaining the shared co-author presentation.

For EirePolitic, Collabs are potentially high-leverage because one piece of content can reach several established audiences without duplicate publication.

However, a Collab visually communicates a closer relationship than a repost or mention.

Therefore:

**use Collabs only where genuine co-ownership/educational partnership is appropriate.**

Do not use Collab invitations merely to borrow follower counts.

## Native reposts

Instagram's native repost feature allows users/accounts to repost public feed posts and Reels.

Meta states that reposted content:

- remains credited to the original creator;
- can appear in the reposter's repost tab;
- may be recommended to that person's followers.

This is lower-friction and lower-affiliation-risk than a Collab.

For many EirePolitic distribution relationships, **repost is preferable to co-authoring**.

## Professional Dashboard

Instagram's Professional Dashboard includes current Best Practices guidance around creation, engagement, reach, and guidelines.

A future implementation chat should check the account's current personalised guidance before assuming older collaboration advice remains optimal.

# Neutrality principle

Distribution choices themselves communicate something about EirePolitic.

A technically factual post can still create an appearance of affiliation if EirePolitic repeatedly co-authors with one party, campaign, ideological group, or advocacy network.

Therefore evaluate both:

1. **the content of the collaboration**; and
2. **the pattern created by the partner portfolio over time**.

The safest growth partners are organisations/accounts whose public role is educational, civic, informational, academic, journalistic, or community-facing rather than electoral/partisan.

# Partnership modes

Do not treat every relationship as a Collab.

## D0 — organic redistribution

No outreach.

Another account/user independently:

- sends;
- saves;
- reposts;
- screenshots;
- references EirePolitic.

Manual effort:

**zero.**

This is the ideal scalable distribution behaviour.

## D1 — editorial mention/tag

EirePolitic mentions or tags an account because it is genuinely relevant to the post.

Examples:

- source organisation;
- polling organisation;
- project collaborator;
- institution directly responsible for a public information resource.

Purpose:

context/attribution first, discovery second.

Do not mention accounts solely to trigger notifications and attract attention.

## D2 — targeted repost/share request

A short message is sent to an organisation/account whose audience is directly served by the post.

Example:

> EirePolitic has published a neutral explainer relevant to your audience. If useful, feel free to repost/share it.

Characteristics:

- no obligation;
- no payment;
- no reciprocal-sharing demand;
- post already exists;
- low relationship implication.

This should be the **default active outreach format**.

## D3 — native Collab

The partner is invited as a co-author.

Use when:

- both parties genuinely contributed;
- both are comfortable appearing jointly on the post;
- topic falls clearly within both missions;
- neutrality/affiliation risk is low;
- joint distribution materially improves utility.

Do not use simply because the partner has a large audience.

## D4 — recurring distribution relationship

Examples:

- an education/community account periodically reposts EirePolitic explainers;
- a student society uses selected EirePolitic civic explainers;
- a public-information organisation occasionally co-produces neutral educational material.

Only build this after successful one-off interactions.

Do not create contractual complexity unless the relationship becomes materially valuable.

# Partner ecosystem

The following categories should be investigated using current web/Instagram research before outreach.

Named examples below are **research starting points**, not endorsements or pre-approved collaboration targets.

## A. Official / statutory public-information bodies

Potential characteristics:

- high credibility;
- neutral/institutional remit;
- strong alignment with civic information;
- formal communications processes;
- potentially low likelihood of accepting informal Collab invitations.

Examples to investigate:

### Houses of the Oireachtas — Education and Outreach

The Oireachtas has an education/outreach function that produces parliamentary educational resources and supports public engagement with Parliament.

Potential overlap:

- parliamentary-process explainers;
- Bills/stages educational material;
- glossary-style civic information.

Likely distribution mode:

- source mention;
- occasional repost if useful;
- formal educational engagement rather than casual Instagram Collab.

Reference:

`https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/working-in-parliament/engaging-citizens/education-and-outreach/`

### An Coimisiún Toghcháin / Electoral Commission

The Electoral Commission is an independent statutory body with public-awareness and electoral-education responsibilities.

Potential overlap:

- election-process explainers;
- constituency/electoral-system content;
- voter-information literacy.

This is **not** a default target for Bill/policy content outside its remit.

Reference:

`https://www.electoralcommission.ie/education/`

### Citizens Information

Citizens Information publishes public explanations of Irish government, elections, institutions, and public services.

Potential overlap:

- institutional/process explainers;
- factual civic-information references.

A future chat should verify the current organisation/account structure and whether social collaboration is appropriate before outreach.

## B. Civic-tech / open-data projects

Characteristics:

- strong subject overlap;
- technically literate audience;
- potential mutual value in explaining public records;
- possible overlap/competition with EirePolitic's own products.

Example research lead:

### Oireachtas Connect

Oireachtas Connect describes itself as an independent non-partisan project making Irish political data accessible from official sources.

Potential collaboration topics:

- political-data literacy;
- sourcing/public-record explainers;
- complementary civic-tech resources.

Before outreach, determine:

- current activity;
- actual audience overlap;
- whether the projects are complementary or duplicative;
- whether collaboration would confuse product identity.

Reference:

`https://oireachtasconnect.ie/about`

## C. Universities / academic units

Potential categories:

- political science;
- law;
- public policy;
- journalism;
- data/social-science programmes;
- civic engagement programmes.

Possible content:

- parliamentary process;
- data literacy;
- research/methodology explainers;
- student civic education.

Important distinction:

A university department/institute and an individual academic are different partnership types.

Do not attribute the views of an individual researcher to the institution.

## D. Student societies

Potentially useful because they:

- already organise political/civic discussion;
- can have engaged younger audiences;
- may be willing to redistribute educational explainers;
- often operate with low bureaucracy.

But every society must be screened individually.

Examples found during the October 2026 research pass include:

- UCD Politics and International Relations Society, which publicly describes itself as operating from a position of neutrality/non-party affiliation;
- DCU Politics Society, which publicly describes itself as non-party affiliated/impartial.

These descriptions must be rechecked at the time of outreach because committees, policies, and account activity change.

Likely first distribution mode:

**repost/share request**, not Collab.

## E. Journalism / media accounts

Potential value:

- audience already interested in Irish current affairs;
- journalists may use EirePolitic graphics as reference/context;
- strong discovery potential.

Neutrality risk:

usually lower than party partnerships, but editorial brands have their own identities and viewpoints.

Preferred approach:

- make EirePolitic content easy to cite/repost;
- build awareness among relevant journalists/researchers;
- avoid asking newsrooms to co-author ordinary EirePolitic analysis unless there is a genuine joint project.

A repost/reference from a journalist can be valuable without creating formal affiliation.

## F. Community / local-information accounts

Potential value:

- constituency-specific representative information;
- locally relevant Bills/policy processes;
- strong practical audience relevance.

Best future use:

when EirePolitic has robust constituency/representative products.

Avoid mass-tagging every local account.

## G. Civic / democracy / education organisations

Potentially suitable where their remit is:

- democratic participation;
- public education;
- institutional literacy;
- media/data literacy.

Screen carefully for political advocacy/ideological positioning.

A mission to improve civic participation does not automatically create a neutrality problem; an organisation campaigning for specific political outcomes may.

## H. Issue-specific advocacy organisations

**Higher neutrality risk.**

They may have highly relevant audiences but explicit policy positions.

Default rule:

- do not make issue-advocacy organisations first-wave growth partners;
- ordinary public reposting is fine;
- do not tailor neutral EirePolitic framing to win endorsement from an advocacy organisation;
- use Collabs only after explicit neutrality review and only where the content is educational rather than advocacy-oriented.

## I. Political parties, candidates, elected representatives, campaign organisations

**Do not use these as routine audience-growth collaboration targets.**

EirePolitic will necessarily publish factual material about political actors, and those actors may independently share/repost it.

But planned Collab/repost-request campaigns directed at political parties or politicians create unnecessary affiliation risk.

Allow:

- factual mention/tag where directly relevant and consistently applied;
- independent reposts by political actors;
- correction/source contact where operationally necessary.

Avoid as a growth tactic:

- co-authored party posts;
- “please share this with your followers” outreach to politicians;
- reciprocal distribution agreements;
- preferential access arrangements.

If a future educational project genuinely involves multiple political actors, design it separately with explicit neutrality safeguards rather than treating it as routine Instagram growth.

# Partner screening framework

Before active outreach, create a short partner record.

Do not use a vague “good fit” judgement.

## Required fields

```yaml
partner_id: "..."
name: "..."
platform: instagram
handle: "..."
partner_class: student_society
public_mission: "..."
party_affiliation: none_detected | declared | unclear
advocacy_positioning: low | medium | high | unclear
current_activity: active | inactive | unclear
audience_overlap: "..."
proposed_distribution_mode: repost_request
proposed_content_type: parliamentary_explainer
neutrality_risk: low | medium | high
last_verified_at: "..."
source_urls:
  - "..."
contact_route: instagram_dm | public_email | form | none
status: research
```

Do not store private/personal contact information unnecessarily.

## Screening questions

### Mission fit

- Does the organisation educate/inform people about politics, public institutions, data, law, journalism, or civic participation?
- Is the proposed post directly relevant to its audience?

### Political affiliation

- Is the account formally affiliated with a party, candidate, campaign, or electoral organisation?
- Does it regularly campaign for/against political actors?

Formal party/campaign affiliation normally excludes the account from active growth outreach.

### Advocacy positioning

- Does it advocate specific policy outcomes?
- Would a Collab reasonably be interpreted as EirePolitic endorsing that position?

Advocacy does not make an organisation illegitimate; it simply changes the appropriate distribution mode.

### Editorial standards

- Does the account identify sources?
- Is its recent content materially factual/reliable?
- Is it active/current?

### Audience fit

- Would its audience plausibly value the specific post?

Avoid outreach solely because an account is large.

### Relationship signal

- Would a repost look normal?
- Would a co-author badge imply a stronger relationship than actually exists?

This determines repost vs Collab.

# Neutrality risk levels

## Low

Typical examples:

- public-information body;
- explicitly non-party civic education account;
- neutral academic/public-data project;
- non-party student society with current evidence of neutrality.

Eligible modes:

- repost request;
- occasional Collab where genuinely co-produced;
- recurring educational relationship after testing.

## Medium

Typical examples:

- media/news brand;
- think tank with identifiable policy perspectives;
- issue organisation;
- individual commentator/creator.

Eligible modes:

- organic redistribution;
- source/relevance mention;
- selective repost request;
- Collab only with explicit case-by-case review.

## High

Typical examples:

- political party;
- candidate/campaign;
- explicitly partisan campaign account;
- advocacy relationship where the content would appear to endorse a contested policy outcome.

Default active growth mode:

**none.**

Independent reposts by these accounts do not require EirePolitic to prevent or endorse them.

# Low-volume outreach system

The operating constraint is the user's time.

## Target workload

Initial pilot:

**15-30 minutes once every two weeks**, not daily.

Possible batch:

- review 3 candidate posts;
- select at most 2-3 partner opportunities;
- send at most 2-3 messages;
- record outcome.

If this creates no measurable value after several cycles, stop.

## Outreach queue

Maintain simple states:

```text
candidate
→ approved_for_outreach
→ contacted
→ responded
→ reposted/collab_accepted/declined/no_response
→ measured
→ reusable_relationship / archive
```

Do not repeatedly message non-responsive accounts.

## Contact frequency

Default rule:

- one initial message;
- one follow-up only when genuinely appropriate;
- then stop.

No automated repeated DMs.

# Outreach message principles

Keep messages short.

A good outreach message communicates:

- what the post is;
- why it is specifically relevant to the recipient's audience;
- that EirePolitic is factual/non-partisan where useful;
- the single requested action;
- no obligation.

Do not send a long project biography.

## Repost-request structure

```text
Hello — EirePolitic has published a short neutral explainer on [specific topic].

It looks directly relevant to [specific reason connected to their audience]. If useful, you're welcome to repost/share it: [post].

Sources/methodology: [where appropriate].
```

Final wording should be adapted naturally, not spammed identically at scale.

## Collab-request structure

Only for genuine joint relevance:

```text
We're preparing a neutral explainer on [specific subject]. It overlaps directly with [partner's educational/public-information work].

Would you be interested in contributing/reviewing [defined part] and publishing it as an Instagram Collab? No problem if not.
```

Do not invite someone as a collaborator after the content is completely finished merely to gain access to their audience; use a repost request instead.

---

# Collaboration standard

A Collab should have a documented reason for joint authorship.

Valid reasons include:

- partner supplied a dataset/expertise component;
- partner co-developed the educational explanation;
- jointly commissioned civic-information content;
- both organisations share responsibility for the resource.

Weak reason:

- partner has many followers.

## Before publishing a Collab

Record:

```text
partner_id
collaboration_purpose
contribution
source ownership
editorial responsibility
approval contact
caption attribution
content reuse expectations
neutrality review
```

For substantive joint work, agree the text/visuals before inviting the Instagram Collab.

Use editorial relevance rules.

## Appropriate

- source polling organisation;
- public institution directly referenced;
- genuine contributor;
- collaboration partner;
- organisation whose public resource is explicitly being cited.

## Inappropriate

- tagging every party represented in a chart;
- tagging journalists in hope of coverage;
- tagging politicians because a Bill concerns their portfolio;
- tagging large Irish accounts unrelated to the post;
- repeated notification-generating tags.

Tags should remain truthful metadata/context, not attention hacks.

# Media / journalist awareness

EirePolitic may eventually become useful as a source of reusable political-data graphics.

The low-work strategy is not to pitch every post.

Instead:

1. publish consistently sourced/reusable graphics;
2. maintain a simple public methodology/source page;
3. make contact information available for corrections/media/data enquiries if desired;
4. selectively send unusually relevant posts/data resources to a small number of journalists/researchers;
5. allow organic citation/reposts to grow.

Do not build a press-distribution operation until evidence shows recurring media uptake.

This is a good first active-distribution experiment because:

- audiences are likely interested in political/civic explainers;
- many societies have explicit non-party or broad-discussion remits;

- research 5-8 currently active societies;

- ask for repost/share, not Collab;

# Experiment programme

## DE-001 — targeted repost request pilot

### Question

Can a very small number of relevant outreach messages create measurable non-follower distribution at acceptable manual cost?

### Method

- choose one evergreen civic explainer;
- identify 2-3 low-risk relevant accounts;
- send a brief repost/share request;
- record time spent and outcomes.

### Primary metrics

- partner repost/share occurrence;
- incremental non-follower reach around the post;
- profile visits;
- follows per 1,000 reached;
- manual minutes.

### Stop rule

If several batches produce little/no redistribution or follow conversion, stop active repost outreach.

---

## DE-002 — student society distribution

Use one reusable parliamentary explainer.

Compare performance when:

- published normally;
- selectively redistributed by one or more non-party student societies.

Track:

- reach;
- profile visits;
- follows;
- saves/shares;
- manual time.

Do not infer causation beyond available attribution.

---

## DE-003 — native Collab pilot

Only after finding a genuine low-risk co-production opportunity.

### Question

Does a jointly authored educational Collab materially outperform ordinary publication/repost distribution?

### Requirements

- defined partner contribution;
- editorial agreement;
- low neutrality risk;
- one controlled post.

### Metrics

- total/non-follower reach;
- profile visits;
- follows per 1,000 reached;
- shares/saves;
- manual coordination time.

A Collab that creates significantly more coordination effort than audience value should not become routine.

---

## DE-004 — repost vs Collab

Where the same partner relationship permits both over time, compare:

- simple partner repost;
- true co-authored Collab.

Question:

Does the extra relationship/coordination signal of Collab create enough acquisition lift to justify it?

Do not manufacture identical duplicate posts solely for the experiment.

---

## DE-005 — partner class comparison

After enough observations, compare descriptive performance by partner class:

- student/academic;
- civic education;
- public-information;
- media/research;
- community/local.

Do **not** rank political actors or organisations by ideology.

The goal is operational:

> which distribution channel yields useful audience at low effort?

---

## DE-006 — opt-in recurring distribution list

Only if repeated partners voluntarily show interest.

Possible model:

Once per month, send an **opt-in** short digest containing 2-3 new reusable EirePolitic explainers.

Do not create unsolicited bulk email/DM distribution.

Success criterion:

partners regularly repost/use content with minimal outreach.

If not, discontinue.

# Measurement model

Record distribution activity against the existing publication identity.

Suggested metadata:

```text
distribution_id
publication_id
partner_id
partner_class
distribution_mode          organic_repost | repost_request | mention | collab | digest
outreach_sent_at
outreach_minutes
response                   accepted | declined | no_response | organic
repost_observed             true/false
collab_accepted             true/false
partner_follower_count_snapshot   optional
neutrality_risk
experiment_id
```

Do not depend heavily on partner follower counts.

Audience size does not equal useful reach, and follower totals change.

## Primary metrics

- non-follower reach;
- profile visits;
- follows per 1,000 reached;
- shares/reposts;
- manual minutes per successful distribution event.

## Efficiency metric

Conceptually:

```text
incremental useful reach / manual outreach minutes
```

and/or

```text
incremental follows / manual outreach minutes
```

Use directionally rather than pretending to have perfect causal attribution.

# Risks and safeguards

## Appearance of political affiliation

The most important risk.

Mitigations:

- exclude parties/campaigns from routine active growth outreach;
- prefer reposts over Collabs when relationship is weak;
- maintain a diversified civic/educational partner portfolio;
- record partner screening and collaboration purpose.

## Partner drift

An organisation/account can change leadership, affiliation, or editorial tone.

Reverify before each new active partnership if significant time has passed.

## Audience-size chasing

Do not trade credibility for a larger account's audience.

Audience relevance and relationship signal matter more than follower count.

## Mass outreach creep

If the workflow starts requiring daily prospecting or many follow-ups, it violates the growth-programme constraints.

Use strict batch limits.

## Hidden endorsement through Collab

A co-author badge can imply mutual endorsement/partnership.

Use only for genuine co-produced educational content.

## Selective political tagging

Repeatedly tagging some parties/politicians but not comparable others can create perceived bias.

Do not use political-actor tagging as a distribution strategy.

## Partner factual error

If a collaborator contributes content, EirePolitic's publication must still pass EirePolitic's sourcing/semantic standards.

Collaboration does not transfer editorial responsibility.

## Causal overclaiming

A post distributed by a partner may perform well because of topic/timing, not the partnership.

Use repeated observations and cautious interpretation.

# Completion criteria

This workstream is mature when:

- a current, compact partner ecosystem map exists;
- partner screening/neutrality rules are documented;
- EirePolitic has tested low-volume repost outreach;
- at least one distribution relationship generates measurable useful audience at low effort, or the workstream has evidence that outreach is not worth continuing;
- Collabs are used only where genuine joint authorship exists;
- political parties/campaigns are not used as routine acquisition partners;
- outreach requires minutes per fortnight/month, not daily work;
- distribution outcomes join to Insights/experiment metadata;
- successful relationships can be reused without repeated prospecting;
- EirePolitic's neutrality remains obvious from both content and partner pattern.

The target end state is **a small network of credible distribution paths that occasionally multiply the reach of EirePolitic's best informational content without turning the project into an outreach operation or political alliance network**.
