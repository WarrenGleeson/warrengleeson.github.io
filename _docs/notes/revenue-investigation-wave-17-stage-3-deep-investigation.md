# Wave 17 Stage 3 — Deep Investigation

## 1. BC tenancy inspection procedural-evidence tracker

### Problem
BC landlords do not only need an inspection record; they need evidence that the inspection process itself was handled correctly. The rules include offering two opportunities, using the approved final-opportunity process where required, completing/signing the report, and delivering the report copy within prescribed timeframes. Procedural failures can affect a landlord's ability to claim against a security or pet damage deposit.

### Product
A lightweight landlord workflow focused on procedure rather than condition capture:
- tenancy/unit record;
- first inspection opportunity/date;
- tenant response/alternative date;
- final-opportunity notice evidence;
- completed inspection/report date;
- signature status;
- copy-delivery method/date;
- move-out forwarding-address date;
- automatic deadline calculation;
- deposit-claim evidence index.

It can attach the official report/photos but does not replace the RTB inspection form or decide whether damage is chargeable.

### Distribution
BC landlord search traffic, landlord associations, bookkeeping/property-management consultants, and a free single-tenancy checklist.

### Monetization hypothesis
Free 1 tenancy; CA$5–10 per completed turnover evidence pack; CA$10–20/month for small landlords/property managers.

### Competition
Move-in/move-out inspection apps are numerous, but they mainly compete on photo capture, guided inspections and condition comparison. The current review did not surface a strong BC-specific product centered on **procedural rights-preservation and evidence deadlines**.

### Risk
Legal sensitivity: the tool must present itself as workflow/evidence administration, not legal advice or a guarantee that a deposit claim will succeed.

### Verdict
**Advance.** Strongest Wave 17 concept.

## 2. Rental turnover utility/key/meter evidence pack

### Problem
Small landlords frequently manage utility cutovers, meter readings, keys/fobs/remotes, rekeying, cleaners and vacancy-period responsibility through email/text/checklists. Failures can create surprise utility bills, service interruptions, missing access devices and weak evidence about who held responsibility when.

### Product
One turnover record per unit:
- move-out/move-in dates;
- utility account responsibility and transfer confirmation numbers;
- meter readings/photos;
- key/fob/remote counts;
- rekey/lock-change completion evidence;
- cleaner/repair completion evidence;
- vacancy-period utility owner;
- exportable tenant/owner handoff pack.

### Distribution
Search around rental turnover checklists, landlord communities and a free printable/digital template.

### Monetization hypothesis
Free one turnover; CA$5–10 per turnover; CA$8–15/month for small portfolios.

### Competition
Turnover checklists are common, but the current search did not identify a strong standalone product focused specifically on evidence-backed utility/access handoffs for very small landlords. Full property-management systems are overkill for this niche.

### Risk
Low willingness to pay and easy substitution with spreadsheets/Notion. The product only works if setup is extremely fast and the evidence export is clearly better than a checklist.

### Verdict
**Advance cautiously.** Broad pain, weaker economics.

## 3. Strata depreciation-report recommendation follow-through register

### Problem
BC strata depreciation reports are mandatory on recurring five-year cycles and contain component inventories, repair/replacement timelines and projected costs. The operational gap begins after the report is delivered: councils need to convert recommendations into actual projects, budget decisions, owner communications and evidence for the next report update.

### Product
Import or manually capture report components/recommendations and track:
- component / recommended year;
- estimated cost;
- priority;
- council decision;
- approved project/resolution;
- vendor quote/contract;
- actual completion date/cost;
- variance from report assumption;
- evidence/photos/invoices;
- notes for the next depreciation-report update.

This does not create or certify a depreciation report.

### Distribution
Self-managed strata search, strata councils, depreciation-report consultants and a free report-to-roadmap template.

### Monetization hypothesis
CA$20–40 one-off report import/setup; CA$10–25/month per strata for ongoing tracking; consultant/referral tier later.

### Competition
Reserve-planning firms and BC strata-management tools increasingly provide living-plan functionality. The surviving wedge is a simple vendor-neutral **report-to-project follow-through register** that can sit beside an existing property manager/report provider.

### Risk
Adjacent platforms may already satisfy enough of this need. Adoption depends on easy import from static PDF reports.

### Verdict
**Advance cautiously.** Useful but more competitive than rank 1.

## Dropped after deeper review

### Generic move-in/move-out inspection app
Crowded with staff-guided, tenant-guided and AI comparison products.

### Commercial CAM reconciliation
Current purpose-built reconciliation/audit tools already address this workflow.

### Generic strata reserve/depreciation planning
Current BC providers and strata platforms already digitize reserve planning and five-year report management.

### Tenant insurance expiry tracking
Common feature in property-management and compliance platforms.

## Stage 3 ranking

1. BC tenancy inspection procedural-evidence tracker
2. Rental turnover utility/key/meter evidence pack
3. Strata depreciation-report recommendation follow-through register

## Advance to Stage 4

Advance those three only.
